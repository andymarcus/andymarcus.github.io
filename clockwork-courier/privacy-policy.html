Applies to the Clockwork Courier Android application (com.clockworkcourier.game)

IN SHORT

The short version

Clockwork Courier has no accounts of its own and no game server. Your progress, keys, settings and unlocked finishes are saved in a few small files on your phone and never leave it. If you use Google Play Games, the achievements you earn are also kept with your Play Games profile.

The advertising and analytics components do collect data. The game shows optional rewarded ads and occasional ads between rooms through Unity LevelPlay, which requests them from ironSource Ads, Unity Ads and Google AdMob and uses device and advertising identifiers to serve and measure them. With your consent, it sends measurements to Google Firebase Analytics under a random app instance ID: which rooms are started, solved or failed and how, which chapters are opened and finished, hints and skips, which optional ads are offered, watched or turned down, what ads earn, and which purchases are completed. That's how I find out which rooms are too hard and whether the ads are fair.

If you buy Remove Ads, a Master Key or Unlimited Keys, Google Play handles the payment and I never see your payment details.

Everything below is the long version of those paragraphs.

02What stays on your device

I do not operate an account system, leaderboard or game server. The game keeps its state in small files in the app's private storage on your phone:

  • Progress: which rooms you've solved, the cogs earned in each, rooms skipped and chapters opened.
  • Wind-up keys: how many you hold and when the next one is due.
  • Settings: sound, music and haptics.
  • Wardrobe: the gear finish and Courier paint you've chosen.
  • Ad pacing: a count of rooms completed since the last ad between rooms.
  • Purchases: a local copy of what you own, so it applies at launch before Google Play replies.

These files are never uploaded, are not backed up to me, and are excluded from Android's app backup. I have no way to read them. Uninstalling the game or clearing its storage deletes them. Reset Progress in the game's settings also wipes your progress.

One distinction is worth stating plainly: the files stay on your phone, but some of what they record (rooms solved, cogs earned, rooms skipped) is also sent as separate analytics events when it happens, described in section 03. Those events carry no name, email or account identifier.

03What third parties collect

The game includes Unity's and Google's advertising components, and Google's consent, analytics, billing and Play Games components. Unity and Google operate these services under their own policies. I receive analytics reports through Firebase and revenue reports from Unity LevelPlay.

Unity LevelPlay, ironSource Ads, Unity Ads and Google AdMob: advertising

Clockwork Courier shows two kinds of ad:

  • Rewarded ads you may choose to watch in exchange for a hint, an extra wind-up key, or skipping a room you're stuck on. They are always optional, and turning one down costs nothing.
  • An occasional full-screen ad between rooms, when you move on from a solved room. Never during the first 10 rooms, at most once every three rooms, never during a run, and never once you own Remove Ads.

The game shows no banner ads.

To serve and measure these, the Unity LevelPlay SDK and the ad networks it requests ads from (ironSource Ads, Unity Ads and Google AdMob, which bid for each ad) may collect and process:

  • your device's advertising identifier (Android Advertising ID);
  • device and app information: model, operating system version, app version, language, network type;
  • approximate location inferred from your IP address (country or region level; the game requests no GPS or location permission);
  • interactions with ads: whether an ad was shown, viewed, completed or clicked.

This is used to choose ads, limit how often you see the same one, measure performance, and detect invalid or fraudulent traffic. ironSource Ads and Unity Ads are both operated by Unity. See Unity's privacy policy for game players and app users, and, for AdMob and the consent message described in section 06, Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses data from apps that use its services.

Google Firebase Analytics: how the game is played

Unity LevelPlay and Google Play already report installs, sessions and revenue without any extra code. Firebase Analytics answers the questions they can't: which rooms players get stuck on or give up at, how far through the four chapters they get, and how often an optional ad is offered and turned down. An ad network only ever sees the offers that were accepted, and refusals are how I find out an offer is annoying rather than useful.

When analytics is allowed (see section 06), the Firebase SDK collects and processes:

  • an app instance identifier: a random string generated on your device so that one player's events count as one player's. It is not your advertising ID, it is not linked to any account, and it is regenerated if you clear the app's data or reinstall;
  • device and app information: model, operating system version, app version, language, network type;
  • approximate location inferred from your IP address (country or region level);
  • the game's own events: a room being started and finished (its number and chapter, whether the Courier delivered or how it failed, the pieces used, the cogs earned and which attempt it was); a chapter being opened or completed; a hint being used, a room skipped, or the keys running out; an optional ad being offered, watched or turned down, and for which purpose (hint, key or skip); the format, network and reported revenue of each ad shown; and which product was bought when a purchase completes;
  • a few events Firebase records by itself, such as first launch, session start and install attribution, which is what the BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE permission in section 05 is for.

What it deliberately does not collect: no free text of any kind, no contacts, no account or email, and no advertising ID. Analytics is built with advertising-ID collection switched off; that identifier belongs to the ads SDK described above. The game sets no user ID and no user properties, and makes no attempt to link any of this to a real identity.

Read Firebase's privacy and security documentation and the list of data Google Analytics for Firebase collects.

Google Play Billing: purchases

The game offers three optional purchases: Remove Ads (stops the ads between rooms; optional rewarded ads stay available because you always choose to start them), the Master Key (24 hours of unlimited wind-up keys) and Unlimited Keys (unlimited wind-up keys for good). Payment is handled entirely by Google Play. I receive only confirmation that a purchase of one of my product identifiers succeeded. I never receive or store your name, card number, billing address or any other payment detail. Restore Purchases in the game's settings asks Google Play what you own; it sends nothing to me.

Google Play Games Services: achievements

The game has 16 achievements through Google Play Games. Play Games connects using your Google gaming profile: it signs you in automatically if you've used Play Games before, and otherwise asks once, on the first launch, and again only if you open Achievements in Settings. When you're connected, the game sends Google your achievement unlocks and progress counts, worked out from your saved progress, and Google keeps them with your Play Games profile. Google may show your gamer name and avatar on its own achievement and profile screens, and other players may see your achievements according to your Play Games profile visibility settings.

There are no leaderboards. The game doesn't read your gamer name, email address, contacts or friends list, and doesn't store your Play Games player ID. Google also collects Play Games SDK analytics and diagnostics to maintain and improve the service; this is separate from the game's Firebase analytics and its ad consent controls. See Google's Play Games data disclosure and Google's Privacy Policy. The game plays the same without a Play Games connection; only achievements need it.

Google Play Services

The game is distributed through Google Play and relies on Google Play Services on your device. Google may process data in connection with app distribution, updates and integrity checks independently of this policy.

06Your choices

Consent, in the EEA, UK and Switzerland

Before any ad is requested, the game shows a Google-certified consent message using Google's User Messaging Platform. You may refuse personalised ads and the game stays fully playable; non-personalised or limited ads may still be available, depending on your choices and the network. You can reopen that choice at any time from Settings → Privacy Options in the game, on the title screen or from the pause menu.

Analytics follows the analytics choice in that message, separately from permission to show ads. Until you allow it, Firebase collection is switched off and every Google consent signal defaults to denied. The few events that happen before you answer are held in memory only: they are sent if you allow analytics, and discarded if you don't or when the app closes. Nothing is written to storage for later sending. Refusing or withdrawing consent switches Firebase analytics off again.

Elsewhere

Where no consent message is required, ads and analytics run as described in section 03. Where Google's consent tool can't confirm your status (for example, on a first launch with no connection), ads and analytics stay off until it can.

On your device, anywhere

  • Opt out of ad personalisation: Android Settings → Google → All services → Ads, then "Delete advertising ID" or "Opt out of Ads Personalisation".
  • Reset your advertising ID in the same place, which disconnects future ad data from past ad data.
  • Reset the analytics app instance ID by clearing the app's data (Android Settings → Apps → Clockwork Courier → Storage → Clear data). This also clears your saved progress. A new identifier is generated and nothing links it to the old one.
  • Stop the ads between rooms by buying Remove Ads. Rewarded ads stay available because you always choose to start them.
  • Delete your game data with Reset Progress in Settings, by clearing the app's storage, or by uninstalling. This doesn't delete achievements already held by Google Play Games.
  • Manage Play Games in Google's Play Games settings: the account used for this game, automatic sign-in and profile visibility. Privacy Options doesn't control Play Games.
  • Delete Play Games data using Google's instructions for deleting data for a game or your Play Games profile. Achievements are worked out from your saved progress, so they're sent again if you reconnect; reset your progress too if you want both gone.

US privacy choices

Ad personalisation may count as a "sale" or "sharing" of personal information, or as targeted advertising, under some US state privacy laws. In the states where those laws apply, Google's consent tool shows a US privacy message, and you can opt out there or later from Settings → Privacy Options.

From version 1.0.4, the game passes an opt-out of sale, sharing or targeted advertising to Unity LevelPlay before any ad is requested, and LevelPlay passes it on to the ad networks it uses. If the game can't read a US choice, it treats it as an opt-out. When you change your choice, ads already loaded under the old one are discarded. The device advertising controls above work everywhere. Section 09 describes your other US rights.

07Legal bases for processing

Where the UK or EU GDPR applies:

  • Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)): personalised advertising and any use of the advertising identifier for it, and Firebase analytics, which is not collected without the consent described in section 06.
  • Contract (Art. 6(1)(b)): delivering and restoring a purchase you have made, and providing the Play Games achievements you choose to use.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)): serving non-personalised ads, capping how often ads appear, and preventing ad fraud.

08Retention and international transfers

The analytics events in section 03 are kept in my Firebase project for 14 months of user-level data, after which Google deletes them automatically. Aggregate reports built from them may be kept longer, in a form that describes players in general and no player in particular. Advertising data held by Unity and Google follows each provider's own retention schedule. Purchase records held by Google Play, and achievements held by Google Play Games, follow Google's schedules.

Because those events carry a randomly generated app instance ID and nothing else, I can't look up "your" data, which is also why I can't delete it on request: there is nothing to match a request against. Clearing the app's data severs the identifier immediately, and the retention window disposes of what came before.

Unity and Google operate globally and may process data outside your country, including in the United States. Their privacy policies describe the safeguards and transfer mechanisms they use.
